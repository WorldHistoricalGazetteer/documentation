If you spot something wrong in a published record, such as a name, a date, a location, or a shape on
the map, you can propose a fix from the record itself.

## Who can suggest, and who decides

- **The owners of each gazetteer decide whether to invite suggestions at all.** Where they have
  not, the option does not appear.
- **You need to be signed in** to make a suggestion.
- **Every suggestion is reviewed by a person** before anything changes. Nothing you propose alters a
  published record automatically.

## Making a suggestion

1. Open the record you want to correct.
2. Choose to suggest a correction. The record opens in an editor, with your changes kept separate from
   the published version.
3. Make the change. If a location or shape is wrong, you can **draw the geometry** on the map.
4. Say briefly why the change is needed, and where the evidence can be found.
5. Submit. You can withdraw a suggestion while it is still under review.

## What happens next

The gazetteer's owners accept or reject each suggestion. Either way it is kept as a permanent record
of who proposed what, when, and on what evidence. It is never silently deleted.

This is how WHG's attestation model works in practice: a correction is itself a sourced, attributed
claim, reviewed before it takes effect.
