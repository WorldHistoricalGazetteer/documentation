The World Historical Gazetteer (WHG) brings together what many sources say about places in the past.
Its index holds roughly **47 million** place records drawn from:

- global reference gazetteers, such as GeoNames, Wikidata, the Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names,
  OpenStreetMap and OpenHistoricalMap;
- specialist historical gazetteers, such as Pleiades and the Vision of Britain and Ireland;
- data contributed by researchers.

Each record keeps its source, its dates and its licence.

WHG v4 is organised around four things you can do.

| You want to… | Use | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Explore** places on a map, through time, across sources | **Atlas** | [Atlas](../../atlas.md) |
| **Match** your own list or table of places to WHG | **Map your Data** | [Map your Data](../../v3-3/map-your-data.md) |
| **Build** a collection, an itinerary or a group of gazetteers, alone or as a team | **Collaborative Workbench** | [Collaborative Collections](../../v3-3/collections.md) |
| **Improve** a published record | **Suggest a correction** | [Suggesting corrections](./corrections.md) |

## Do I need an account?

- **No,** to search, explore Atlas, read place records, and download openly licensed data.
- **Yes,** to save work, collaborate, contribute data, or suggest a correction.

WHG accounts use **ORCiD** to sign in. If you do not have an ORCiD iD, you can create one free at
[orcid.org](https://orcid.org) as part of signing in.

## One place, many sources

The same place is usually described by several sources, each in its own way: different spellings,
scripts, coordinates, dates and categories. WHG does not merge these into one master record.
Each source's statement is kept as a separate, **attributed claim** (an *attestation*), and WHG
shows you where they agree.

- In **Atlas**, records that appear to describe the same place are grouped as you explore. You
  control how cautious that grouping is.
- In **Map your Data**, the same evidence (spelling and sound, location, type and country) is
  used to suggest matches for your rows, with a confidence you can review.

Two features help with names written differently:

- **Sounds-alike matching** finds names that are spelled or scripted differently but sound alike.
  See [Sounds-alike search](./sounds-alike.md).
- **Standardised place types** from the Getty Art & Architecture Thesaurus let you filter by the
  kind of place, whichever source a record came from.
